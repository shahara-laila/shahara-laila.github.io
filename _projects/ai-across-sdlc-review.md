A systematic literature review, conducted following the PRISMA 2020 guidelines, that maps how Artificial Intelligence is applied across the entire software development life cycle (SDLC) — from requirements and design through code generation, testing, maintenance, deployment, and project management.

## Overview

AI has moved from a supporting tool to an active participant in software engineering, yet existing reviews are usually bounded by a single lifecycle phase or a single technique. This review synthesizes the evidence as a whole to reveal imbalances that are invisible from within any one phase, answering three questions: how AI is applied across the SDLC, which techniques dominate, and what benefits and challenges are reported.

## Method

- PRISMA 2020 systematic review protocol
- Searched five digital libraries: IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, Scopus, SpringerLink, ScienceDirect
- 1,124 records → 892 after duplicate removal → 50 peer-reviewed studies (2022–2026) after screening and full-text eligibility
- Five-item quality instrument applied against a pre-defined protocol

## Key Findings

- Adoption is markedly uneven: implementation and testing dominate, while design, project management, and especially deployment and operations remain under-examined
- Methods are converging on a single foundation model applied across phases, with the phase specified through the prompt rather than the architecture
- Reported gains cluster around productivity while costs cluster around correctness, security, and maintainability — the two are rarely measured together
- Sets out five research directions: autonomous/multi-agent SE, explainable and trustworthy assistance, secure AI-generated software, human–AI collaboration, and benchmarking, evaluation and governance
